How to clean, shape, store and measure a cowboy hat

The complete care guide from the hat wall in Caldwell. How to keep a good hat looking right for years, plus how to size one and why the good ones cost what they do.

A good cowboy hat is one of the few things you can buy that gets better the more you wear it, as long as you look after it. Most hats do not wear out, they get handled to death. Below is everything we tell folks at the hat wall, from sizing a new one to cleaning, shaping, stretching and storing the hat you already love. Skip to the part you need, or read it all once and your next hat will outlast a whole shelf of cheap ones.

How to measure your cowboy hat size

Start with the size, because a hat that does not fit will never feel right no matter how good it looks. Wrap a soft tape measure around your head about a half inch above your ears and eyebrows, right where the hat sits. Keep it snug and level, not squeezed. That measurement in inches or centimeters matches to a hat size on any sizing chart. Your head is a slight oval, not a circle, so a quality hat is shaped to match, and the fit you want is even all the way around with no pinch at the temples and no gap at the back. When you land between sizes, go up and fine tune the fit with a sizing tape tucked inside the sweatband. That is a small adjustment on a hat that already sits close, not a fix for the wrong size.

Felt or straw: which hat to buy

The everyday question at the wall is felt or straw, and the answer is the season, not the style. Straw is for heat. It breathes, it is light, and it is what you want for summer work, arena days, and long hours under a hard sun. Felt is for cold and for dressing up. It blocks wind, sheds a little weather, and carries the finish you want at a wedding, a sale, or a Friday night in town. Plenty of folks keep one of each and grab whichever the day calls for. Buying just one to start? Buy for the season you spend the most time outside in, and you will get more wear out of it.

How to clean a cowboy hat

Cleaning depends on the material, so treat felt and straw differently. For a felt hat, knock the loose dust off first with a soft hat brush, always brushing counterclockwise around the crown so you work with the nap instead of against it. Light marks and scuffs lift with a dry sponge or a soft gum eraser worked in small strokes. Keep water off felt whenever you can, because soaking flattens the nap and can leave a ring. For a straw hat, a wipe with a barely damp cloth is enough, followed by a dry cloth. Whatever the material, never scrub, and never reach for household cleaners. A deep set stain or a sweat line all the way through the felt is a job for a hat shop with the right tools, and it is worth the trip before a favorite hat is ruined by a wet rag at the kitchen sink.

How to shape or reshape a cowboy hat

The crown crease and the brim are where a hat becomes yours, and none of it is permanent on a quality hat. To reshape felt, warm it with steam from a kettle or a garment steamer until the felt is warm and pliable, then work the crown crease or roll the brim with your hands and hold it until it cools and sets. Small changes take seconds, and you can snap a brim up, open a crown, or set a cleaner cattleman crease this way. Straw is more delicate and holds a shape best with light steam and patience, so go gentle. If any of that feels like a gamble on a hat you love, most western stores will steam and shape it for you in a few minutes, and we do it here at no charge on hats folks buy from us.

How to stretch a cowboy hat that is too tight

A snug hat that gives you a headache by lunch is usually a size, not a life sentence. A little steam around the sweatband softens the fibers so the hat gives as it dries, either on your own head or on a hat stretcher set a touch larger. A proper wooden or ratchet hat stretcher left in overnight is the safest, most even way to gain a size, and it beats yanking on the brim. Work in small steps and check the fit as you go, because a hat that gets stretched too far will not shrink back down without more trouble than it is worth. If you are close by, bring it in and we will stretch it on the shop stretcher so you do not overdo it.

How to store a cowboy hat so it keeps its shape

Storage is where most good hats quietly go bad. Two rules do almost all the work. First, pick a hat up and set it down by the brim, never by pinching the front of the crown, because that pinch is how creases turn into cracks and dents. Second, when it is off your head, rest it upside down on its crown or hang it on a peg or hat rack so the brim keeps its shape instead of flattening on a shelf. Keep it dry, keep it out of a hot truck or off a radiator, and give a felt hat a quick brush now and then. Heat is the real enemy in storage, because it warps the shape, so a cool, dry spot away from the window is all a hat asks for.

Why cowboy hats are so expensive

Once you have shopped a wall of hats, the price spread makes sense. Cost mostly tracks the felt. Wool felt is the affordable, hard wearing choice and a smart place to start. Fur felt, and the blends that climb up the percentages of beaver and rabbit, hold their shape better, shed weather better, and last far longer, which is why a good one can be a lifetime hat instead of a two season one. Past the felt, the quiet details add up: a real leather sweatband is more comfortable and lasts far longer than a synthetic one, and even stitching and clean brim binding are the marks of a hat built to hold up. You do not need the priciest hat on the wall. You need honest materials and honest construction, and once someone shows you where to look, the value is easy to feel with your own hands.

Quick answers

Measure your size: tape around the head a half inch above the ears and brow, snug and level, then match a sizing chart.
Clean felt: brush counterclockwise, lift marks with a dry sponge, keep water off.
Clean straw: wipe with a barely damp cloth, then dry.
Reshape: steam the felt warm, work the crease by hand, let it cool.
Stretch a tight hat: light steam plus a hat stretcher overnight, in small steps.
Store it: handle by the brim, rest upside down or on a rack, keep it cool and dry.
